Specialist depth
CFS

Certified Functional Specialist

Level 3

Level 3 (Analytical) capability in one specialism: diagnosing problems, choosing between methods, interpreting incomplete or ambiguous information and justifying recommendations.

Seven specialisations
1Demand & Supply Planning
2Supply Chain Analytics
3Production and Operations Planning
4Integrated Business Planning
5Supply Chain Network Design
6Supply Chain Cost Management
7Quality and Supplier Performance
Assessment principle
  • Scenario-based analysis and applied reasoning mapped to competency units
  • Recall-only examinations cannot establish Level 3
  • The credential specification lists the exact units assessed
Suitable for
  • Practitioners on a technical career path
  • Professionals seeking formal evidence of domain depth
  • Managers validating a new specialism
  • Employers setting role-specific standards

End-to-end practice
CP

Certified Professional

Level 3

Applying end-to-end supply chain discipline, analysing core domains and understanding cross-functional effects. Typically manages processes, projects, teams, suppliers, plans, customer commitments or operational performance.

Nine specialisations
1Procurement & Sourcing
2Logistics & Transportation
3Inventory & Warehousing
4Global Trade & Compliance
5Contract and Commercial Management
6Distribution and Channel Management
7Freight and Carrier Management
8Sustainable and Green Supply Chain
9Supply Chain Project Management
The credential demonstrates
  • Reliable use of established methods
  • Analysis of service, cost, inventory, supplier, risk and data implications
  • Identification of cross-domain causes, not symptoms
  • Ethical, commercially defensible recommendations
  • Sound use of data, assumptions and professional judgement
Suitable for
  • Supply chain, procurement, planning, inventory, logistics and operations professionals
  • Team leaders and process owners
  • Professionals moving to broader accountability
  • Employers wanting a common end-to-end benchmark

Chartered tier
ChSCP

Chartered Supply Chain Professional

Level 4

Level 4 (Architectural) competence across most of the framework, integrating strategy, operations, finance, information and trust.

Architectural competence includes
1Designing networks, operating models, policies, processes or governance systems
2Reframing problems that are only symptoms
3Making trade-offs explicit across service, cost, cash, risk, sustainability and stakeholders
4Defending recommendations in commercial terms
5Turning analysis into decisions, adoption and lasting change
6Showing judgement under uncertainty and conflicting evidence
Evidence route
  • Level 4 cannot be established by selected-response exams alone
  • Combines advanced case analysis or defence, portfolio evidence, professional interview and verified experience
  • As set out in the approved certification handbook
Suitable for
  • Supply chain directors and senior functional leaders
  • Transformation, network, planning, procurement, logistics or risk architects
  • Cross-functional programme leaders
  • Professionals accountable for enterprise design
The Chartered designation is a private professional recognition and must not be presented as a governmental or statutory charter. Title use follows the current award and membership policy.

No mandatory preparation route

CISCM defines competence, not one required route. Prepare through employment, self-study, employer programmes, academic study, recognised training providers — or a mix. The certification handbook's eligibility, assessment and evidence rules take precedence.

Assessment principles

Rigorous, proportionate, fair

  • No assessment tests beyond the published Body of Knowledge.
  • Item difficulty matches the claimed proficiency.
  • Domain weightings follow the Examination Content Outline tolerance.
  • Higher-level competence needs evidence beyond recall and multiple choice.
  • Ethical breaches cannot be offset by strong technical reasoning.
  • Candidates must judge the meaning and reliability of data, not just calculate.
  • Financial framing is expected where strategic or architectural judgement is required.
Outcome Suitable methods Key controls
Knowledge and understanding Scenario MCQs, short answers, oral questioning Blueprint, item review, difficulty balance, answer key, security
Applied analysis Case analysis, data interpretation, sourcing, inventory or logistics problems Authentic information, stated assumptions, analytical rubric, moderation
Practical skill Observation, simulation, demonstration, system tasks, workplace evidence Identity checks, trained assessors, repeatable conditions, checklist
Integrated competence Project, portfolio, presentation, professional discussion, capstone Milestones, authenticity checks, triangulation, rubric, independent sampling
Ethics and judgement Dilemmas, scenario justification, reflection, professional discussion Criteria based on reasoning, duties, evidence and impact
Assessment integrity: candidates are told the conditions, permitted resources, pass rules, grading, reassessment, submission and appeals. Any permitted AI use is stated and must be disclosed under the task rules.
