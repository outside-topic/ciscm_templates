Frequently Asked Questions

CISCM helps professionals, employers and education providers turn supply chain knowledge into demonstrable workplace capability.

About CISCM

General

The Chartered Institute of Supply Chain Management is a Texas-registered independent professional organization specializing in supply chain, logistics, procurement and operations management. CISCM develops professional standards, credentials, learning and recognition services designed to improve workplace capability and responsible supply chain performance.
Body of Knowledge & Credentials

Certification & Assessment

The CISCM Body of Knowledge defines the assessable scope of CISCM certification and the competence framework used for membership, continuing professional development, provider mapping and employer capability work. It explains not only what professionals should know, but what they should be able to do.

The framework includes:

  • Four Currents—Material, Money, Information and Trust
  • Fourteen domains covering the end-to-end discipline
  • Seventy-two specific competency units
  • Five cumulative proficiency levels
  • Six cross-cutting threads

Every competency unit in the Second Edition includes explanatory commentary, knowledge elements, observable performance criteria, an extended worked example and three characteristic failure modes.

Every CISCM assessment item or evidence requirement maps to a stated competency unit and targets a defined proficiency level. Assessment methods are selected based on the outcome being measured:

  • Knowledge and understanding—Scenario MCQs, short responses, oral questioning
  • Applied analysis—Case analysis, data interpretation, problem-solving
  • Practical skill—Observation, simulation, demonstration, workplace evidence
  • Integrated competence—Project, portfolio, presentation, professional discussion
  • Ethics and judgement—Dilemma, scenario justification, reflection, professional discussion

Higher-level competence requires evidence beyond recall and selected-response tests. No assessment tests outside the published Body of Knowledge.

Accreditation

Accreditation

Accreditation is a CISCM decision that reviewed requirements are met for the exact legal entity, site, program, mode, language, territory and validity stated in the approval record.

CISCM accreditation must not be described as governmental approval, university accreditation, academic degree authority, statutory licensure, immigration eligibility, guaranteed credit transfer or regulator recognition unless an independent competent authority has expressly confirmed that status.

  • Institutional Accreditation
  • Program Accreditation
  • Accredited Corporate Academy
  • Approved Training Provider
  • Approved Delivery Centre
  • Related trainer, assessor, moderator or CPD recognition services
Membership & CPD

Membership & Continuing Professional Development

  • Associate Member—Person with relevant learning and at least one year of relevant experience, or accepted competence evidence
  • Professional Member—Person with relevant competence and at least three years of progressive experience
  • Chartered Member—Experienced professional with advanced competence, significant accountability, ethical standing and peer-reviewed evidence
  • Fellow—Senior professional with sustained contribution, impact, leadership and professional service, normally over eight to ten years

Professional grades should normally complete at least 30 relevant CPD hours per year, including at least 15 structured hours. At least two hours should address ethics, compliance, sustainability, responsible sourcing, data or related professional responsibility. The final requirement must follow the approved grade policy.

Courses, webinars, workplace projects, research, mentoring, teaching, writing, conferences, professional service and guided self-study may count where they are relevant, evidenced and connected to an identified development need.
Governance & Obligations

Verification, Governance & Member Obligations

CISCM recognition is governed by evidence, impartial review, controlled certification, accurate public claims, continuing monitoring and access to complaint and appeal processes. Recognition is limited to the named person, institution, program, site, mode, territory and validity shown in the official decision and verification record.

Members are expected to:

  • act honestly, competently, lawfully and fairly
  • protect confidential and personal information
  • declare and manage conflicts of interest
  • avoid bribery, corruption, fraud, discrimination, exploitation and misleading claims
  • maintain relevant professional competence
  • use designations and marks exactly as authorized
  • disclose material professional or disciplinary matters relevant to standing
  • cooperate with verification, complaint and disciplinary procedures
  • keep subscription and contact information current
