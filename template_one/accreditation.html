Accreditation overview

CISCM's decision that published requirements are met

Accreditation checks whether a defined institution or programme meets CISCM's published professional-learning and quality requirements — for a specific legal entity, site, programme, delivery mode, language, territory and validity period.

  • Time-limited recognition
  • Limited to a specific, stated scope
  • Monitored throughout its validity
  • Publicly verifiable on the CISCM register

Unless an independent competent authority confirms it, accreditation must not be presented as:

  • Government approval
  • University accreditation
  • Academic degree authority
  • Statutory licensure
  • Immigration eligibility
  • Guaranteed credit transfer
  • Regulator recognition

Standard accreditation lifecycle

Ten stages, from enquiry to renewal

1

Enquiry and eligibility screening

2

Application, declaration, exact scope and fee confirmation

3

Completeness check and risk classification

4

Evidence review and clarification

5

Interviews, observation, sampling or validation visit

6

Draft findings and factual-accuracy response

7

Corrective action for any nonconformity

8

Independent decision and scope confirmation

9

Agreement, award letter, certificate, register entry and authorised claim

10

Monitoring, annual return, change notification, renewal or closure

Readiness check — you are ready when:

  • Legal identity, ownership, authority and operating names are consistent
  • Programmes, sites, modes, languages, territories and partners are precisely defined
  • Controlled policies and current operating evidence are available
  • An accountable coordinator can manage the process
  • Marketing claims match current authority
  • Leadership accepts possible conditions, monitoring, corrective action and refusal

Institutional accreditation

Recognition of the quality system behind professional learning

Checks whether an organisation has working governance and quality controls to design, deliver, assess and improve professional learning within an approved scope.

Training providers and professional institutesCorporate academiesColleges and university unitsConsulting and training firmsNonprofit learning organisationsGovernment or sector training entitiesOther legally established learning organisations
Category Intended use Typical approved scope
Accredited Training Institution Established multi-programme organisation with documented quality systems Named sites, delivery modes, territories, subject domains
Accredited Corporate Academy Internal academy for employees or approved ecosystem participants Defined workforce, business units, corporate programmes
Approved Training Provider Authorised to deliver defined CISCM or recognised programmes Named programmes, trainers, locations, modes
Approved Delivery Centre Site operating under an accountable parent or provider Named location with defined administrative and delivery authority

Policies alone are not enough — evidence must show they work across the requested scope.

Validation activity

  • Interview leaders, staff, trainers, learners and partners
  • Trace learner journeys
  • Review governance and assessment
  • Demonstrate systems and inspect facilities
  • Observe delivery and sample records
  • Compare public claims with actual authority

Decision and validity

3 years

recommended, with annual surveillance. Shorter periods or added monitoring may apply. The approval letter and register are the final authority.

Outcomes

A scope-specific decision and controlled certificate, register entry, authorised accreditation statement and mark, independent findings and improvement priorities, and eligibility for published partner opportunities.

Program accreditation

Independent review of programme design, delivery, assessment and outcomes

An independent review of one defined learning programme — on its own or alongside institutional accreditation. The decision covers only the approved title, version, level, mode, language, sites, partners and credential.

Programme review outcomes

AccreditedAll material criteria are met.
Accredited with conditionsLimited gaps can be fixed without unacceptable risk.
Developmental recognitionPromising design; needs stronger evidence, pilot results or assessment.
DeferredEssential evidence, a sample, a pilot or corrective action is outstanding.
RefusedMaterial criteria remain unmet, or risk is unacceptable.

Assessment expectations

Every outcome is assessed at the intended complexity; conditions, pass rules, permitted resources, reassessment and appeals are transparent; remote or AI-assisted work has proportionate identity, authorship and disclosure controls; moderation samples assessors, sites, modes, result bands and borderline decisions.

Change control

Material changes — title, award, outcomes, learning time, assessment, pass standard, language, partners, ownership, audience, entry requirements, level, delivery mode or substantial curriculum — normally need prior approval. Minor updates go in the programme change log.
